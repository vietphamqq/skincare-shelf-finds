The short answer: apply your products from thinnest texture to thickest, finishing with sunscreen in the morning. Cleanser comes first, watery serums next, creams after that, and SPF (or a richer night moisturizer) seals everything in. Once you know that one rule, almost every routine question answers itself.

## The thin-to-thick rule

Skincare layering follows a simple logic. Lightweight, watery products are designed to sink in quickly, so they go on first, when your skin can absorb them easily. Heavier creams and oils sit closer to the surface and can slow down whatever you apply on top of them, so they go last.

If you ever forget the exact order, just hold two products in your hands and ask: which one is runnier? That one goes first. Gel before lotion, lotion before cream, cream before oil.

The one exception to "thin to thick" is sunscreen. It always goes last in the morning, no matter its texture, because it needs to form an even film on top of your skin to do its job.

## Your morning order

Mornings are about protection, so the routine can stay short:

1. **Cleanser.** A gentle, non-stripping face wash — or just a rinse with water if your skin is dry and you cleansed well the night before.
2. **Antioxidant serum (optional).** Many people use a vitamin C or other antioxidant serum here. It's commonly used to help skin look brighter and more even over time. If you use one, apply it to clean, dry skin.
3. **Moisturizer.** Match the weight to your skin: a light gel for oily skin, a richer cream for dry skin.
4. **Sunscreen.** A broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, applied generously as the final step. This is the one product most worth doing every single day.

That's it. A morning routine doesn't need to be longer than this, and for many people steps 1, 3, and 4 alone make a complete routine.

## Your evening order

Nighttime is when treatment products usually go on, since some actives don't pair well with sun exposure and your skin isn't competing with makeup or SPF:

1. **Cleanse — or double cleanse.** If you wore sunscreen or makeup, many people find a two-step cleanse works best: an oil-based cleanser or balm first to dissolve SPF and makeup, then a gentle water-based cleanser to finish. If you wore neither, one gentle cleanse is plenty.
2. **Treatment actives.** This is where a retinoid, an exfoliating acid, or another leave-on treatment goes — applied to clean, dry skin. Pick one active per night rather than stacking several; more is not better. When you introduce any new active, patch test it on a small area first and start with two or three nights a week.
3. **Moisturizer.** A fragrance-free moisturizer locks in hydration and helps buffer any dryness from your actives. On nights when your skin feels irritated, it's completely fine to skip the active and just cleanse and moisturize.

## Where eye cream and spot treatments fit

**Eye cream** follows the same thin-to-thick rule. If yours is lightweight, apply it before moisturizer; if it's thick and balmy, apply it after. Honestly, either order works fine — consistency matters far more than sequence here. And if your regular moisturizer is gentle enough to use around the eyes, a separate eye cream is optional, not mandatory.

**Spot treatments** go on clean skin, directly on the spot, before moisturizer — so the treatment touches the blemish rather than sitting on a layer of cream. Some people prefer to dab them on after moisturizer to reduce dryness in the surrounding skin; both approaches are common. Just avoid layering a spot treatment and a strong exfoliating acid on the same area in the same night.

## Do you need to wait between steps?

Mostly, no. For everyday hydrating serums and moisturizers, you can layer as soon as the previous product feels spread evenly — no timer required.

A short wait genuinely helps in a few cases:

- **Before a retinoid.** Applying it to fully dry skin — a minute or two after cleansing — is a common way to reduce the chance of stinging.
- **Before sunscreen.** Let your moisturizer settle for a moment so the SPF can form an even layer instead of mixing into a slippery film.
- **Before makeup.** Give sunscreen a few minutes to set so it doesn't pill under foundation.

If a product pills or balls up when you apply the next one, that's your cue to slow down, use less, or wait a beat longer.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Applying sunscreen before moisturizer.** SPF is always the last skincare step in the morning.
- **Stacking multiple actives at once.** A retinoid plus an exfoliating acid in the same routine is a classic recipe for irritation, especially for beginners.
- **Using too much of everything.** A pea-sized amount of treatment product and a nickel-sized amount of moisturizer is usually plenty. Extra product doesn't absorb — it just pills.
- **Skipping moisturizer because your skin is oily.** Oily skin still benefits from hydration; choose a light gel texture instead of skipping the step. If you're not sure what your skin needs, our [skin types]({{ '/skin-types/' | relative_url }}) guides can help you figure that out.
- **Chasing a ten-step routine.** More steps mean more chances for products to conflict. A short routine done daily beats an elaborate one done occasionally.

If your skin stays irritated, or a persistent concern isn't improving with a consistent routine, see a board-certified dermatologist — that's what they're there for.

## The short version

- Layer thin to thick: cleanser, then serums, then moisturizer.
- Morning: cleanse, optional antioxidant serum, moisturizer, then broad-spectrum SPF 30+ last — always.
- Night: cleanse (double cleanse if you wore SPF or makeup), one treatment active, then moisturizer.
- Eye cream goes wherever its texture fits; spot treatments go on clean skin before moisturizer.
- Waiting between steps is mostly unnecessary — except letting skin dry before a retinoid and letting sunscreen set.
- Patch test new actives, use one at a time, and keep the routine short enough to do every day.

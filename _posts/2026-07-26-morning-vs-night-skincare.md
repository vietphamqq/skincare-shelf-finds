The simplest way to split your routine: morning is for protecting your skin from the day ahead, and night is for treating and repairing it. Sunscreen and antioxidants belong to the morning; retinoids and exfoliating acids belong to the night. Cleansing and moisturizing belong to both.

## Why the split exists at all

Your skin faces different conditions across a 24-hour day. In the daytime, it's exposed to UV light, wind, and whatever else the outside world throws at it. At night, it's resting — no sun, no makeup, no SPF film — which makes evening the natural window for leave-on treatments that need uninterrupted time on the skin.

There's also a practical reason for the split: some popular actives, like certain retinoids and exfoliating acids, can make skin more sensitive to sunlight. Using them at night and being diligent with sunscreen in the morning is the standard, sensible pairing.

None of this means your skin only "repairs itself at night" in some magical way. It simply means each half of the day plays to a different strength.

## What belongs in the morning: protect

The morning routine has one non-negotiable job — getting sunscreen on your face — and everything else supports it.

- **A gentle cleanse.** A quick wash, or even just a splash of water if your skin runs dry. You're removing overnight product residue, not deep cleaning.
- **An antioxidant serum (optional).** Vitamin C is the classic example. Antioxidant serums are commonly used in the morning because they're thought to complement sunscreen, and many people find them helpful for a brighter, more even look over time.
- **Moisturizer.** Light enough to sit comfortably under SPF.
- **Broad-spectrum SPF 30+.** The final step, applied generously. Daily sunscreen is widely considered the single most useful habit in skincare, both for how skin looks now and how it ages.

If you only do two things in the morning, make them moisturizer and sunscreen.

## What belongs at night: treat and repair

Evening is when treatment products earn their keep, because they get hours of contact with clean, bare skin.

- **A proper cleanse.** If you wore sunscreen or makeup, a double cleanse — an oil or balm cleanser first, then a gentle water-based one — removes it more thoroughly than a single wash.
- **One treatment active.** This is the slot for a retinoid (commonly used to help the look of fine lines and uneven texture) or an exfoliating acid like glycolic, lactic, or salicylic acid (commonly used to help skin look smoother and clearer). One active per night is the rule — pick your priority.
- **Moisturizer.** Often a slightly richer one than you'd wear in the morning, since there's no makeup or SPF to layer over it. It also helps buffer the dryness that actives can bring.

Whenever you introduce a new active, patch test it on a small area for a few days first, and start slow — two or three nights a week is plenty in the beginning.

## What belongs in both routines

Two steps show up morning and night, every day:

- **Cleansing** — though the evening version does the heavier lifting.
- **Moisturizing** — hydrated skin simply behaves better, whatever your skin type.

Gentle hydrating serums (like a hyaluronic acid serum) are also fine in either routine, or both. They're not actives in the strong sense, so they don't need the same caution.

## What not to double up on

A few things genuinely don't need to appear twice a day — and doubling them often backfires:

- **Retinoids.** Once a day at most, at night. Twice daily just multiplies irritation.
- **Exfoliating acids.** Using them morning and night is one of the fastest routes to a red, stinging face. A few nights a week is enough for most people.
- **Strong cleansers.** If your skin feels tight and squeaky after washing, you're cleansing too hard, too often, or with too harsh a formula.
- **Multiple actives in one routine.** A retinoid and an exfoliating acid on the same night is a lot to ask of most skin. Alternate nights instead.

The pattern behind all of these: irritation is the enemy of consistency. A routine you can repeat comfortably every day will beat an aggressive one you have to keep taking breaks from.

## The minimalist versions

If a multi-step routine isn't happening, here's the honest minimum that still covers the fundamentals.

**Minimal morning:**

1. Rinse or gently cleanse
2. Moisturizer (a fragrance-free one keeps things simple)
3. Broad-spectrum SPF 30+

**Minimal night:**

1. Cleanse (double cleanse if you wore SPF or makeup)
2. Moisturizer

That's a complete, respectable routine. Actives are the optional layer you add on top once the basics are automatic — one at a time, at night, a few times a week. For a deeper walkthrough of layering and order, see our [routine basics]({{ '/routine-basics/' | relative_url }}) guides.

And a note worth repeating: skincare products are cosmetic tools. If you're dealing with persistent breakouts, redness, or any skin concern that isn't budging, see a board-certified dermatologist rather than adding more products to the pile.

## The short version

- Morning = protect: gentle cleanse, optional antioxidant serum, moisturizer, then broad-spectrum SPF 30+ last.
- Night = treat: thorough cleanse, one treatment active (retinoid or exfoliating acid), then moisturizer.
- Cleansing, moisturizing, and gentle hydrating serums belong in both routines.
- Don't double up on retinoids, exfoliating acids, or harsh cleansers — irritation undoes progress.
- The minimalist version — moisturizer and SPF in the morning, cleanse and moisturize at night — is a genuinely complete routine.
- Patch test new actives, add them slowly, and see a dermatologist for anything persistent.

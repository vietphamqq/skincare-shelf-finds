## What the Routine Builder does

The idea is simple. You pick your skin type, tap the steps you actually use — cleanser, vitamin C, retinoid, moisturizer, sunscreen, and so on — into a morning column and an evening column. As you build, two things happen:

- **The tool flags common pairing questions.** Add a retinoid and an AHA exfoliant to the same evening, and it will gently note that these are commonly split onto alternate nights to keep irritation down. Forget sunscreen in the morning, and it will remind you that SPF is the one step almost every guide agrees on.
- **A routine card builds itself as you go.** When you're happy, you download it as a single clean image — sized for printing, saving to your phone, or sticking on the bathroom mirror.

The pairing notes reflect commonly published usage guidance — the same kind of advice you'll find in our [guide to ingredients you shouldn't mix]({{ '/ingredients/ingredients-you-shouldnt-mix/' | relative_url }}) — not medical rules. Skin tolerance is individual, and the instructions on your specific product always win.

## Why write your routine down at all?

Most routines fail for a boring reason: they only exist in your head. That leads to three familiar problems.

**Order drifts.** The sequence you apply products in affects how well they work — dermatologists generally recommend going from thinnest to thickest texture, which is why a written order beats improvising at the sink. If you've ever wondered whether serum goes before or after moisturizer, writing the routine down once settles it permanently.

**Actives pile up.** When nothing is written down, it's easy to end up using a vitamin C serum, an exfoliating acid, and a retinoid in the same 24 hours without noticing. Spacing actives across the week — retinoid nights, exfoliation nights, recovery nights — is much easier when you can see the whole week at once.

**New products jump the queue.** A written routine gives every new purchase a simple test: which slot does this fill? If there's no empty slot, maybe the shelf doesn't need it — your wallet will thank you.

## How to build a routine that sticks

A few principles we baked into the tool, which hold true whether you use it or not:

**Start smaller than you think.** A three-step routine you repeat every day beats a nine-step routine you abandon by Thursday. Cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen in the morning; cleanser, treatment, moisturizer at night. That's a complete routine.

**One new active at a time.** Introduce a single new active ingredient, use it two or three times a week at first, and give your skin a couple of weeks before adding anything else. If your skin stings or flakes, drop back to the basics and rebuild slowly.

**Morning is for protection, evening is for treatment.** Antioxidants like vitamin C plus sunscreen in the morning; retinoids and exfoliants at night. This single rule resolves most "can I use these together?" questions before they come up.

**Sunscreen is non-negotiable.** Health authorities recommend broad-spectrum sunscreen as part of everyday sun protection — and most actives in your routine make consistent SPF even more important, since ingredients like retinoids and exfoliating acids can increase sun sensitivity.

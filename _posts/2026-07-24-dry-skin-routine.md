Dry skin does best with a routine built around one idea: protect the barrier. That means a cream cleanser that doesn't strip, hydration layered onto damp skin, a moisturizer to seal it in, and sunscreen every morning — while cutting out the everyday habits that quietly dry skin out.

## Think barrier first

Your skin barrier is the outermost layer of skin — a wall of cells and natural lipids whose job is to keep water in and irritants out. When skin is dry, that wall tends to be short on lipids, so water escapes faster than it should. The result is the familiar dry-skin feeling: tightness after washing, flaking, rough patches, and makeup that clings unevenly.

Every choice below follows from this. Dry skin doesn't need aggressive treatment; it needs less taken out and more put back in.

## Start with a cream cleanser

Cleansing is where dry skin routines most often go wrong. Foaming cleansers with a squeaky finish can take your skin's own lipids along with the day's grime — exactly what dry skin can't spare.

A cream, milk, or balm cleanser cleans without that stripped feeling. Use lukewarm water, massage for about thirty seconds, and rinse. Your face should feel soft afterward, not tight. If it feels tight, the cleanser is too harsh — full stop.

In the morning, you may not need a cleanser at all. Many people with dry skin find a rinse with lukewarm water is plenty, saving the cleanser for evening.

## Layer your hydration

This is the heart of a dry skin routine, and the order matters.

**Step 1: a humectant serum on damp skin.** Humectants — ingredients like glycerin and hyaluronic acid — draw water into the skin. They work best when there's water around to grab, which is why applying them to slightly damp skin is the classic move. After cleansing, don't towel off completely; leave skin a little dewy, then press in your serum.

**Step 2: a cream moisturizer on top.** A humectant alone isn't enough — in dry air, water can evaporate right back out. Follow the serum with a cream moisturizer to seal the hydration in. Look for creams that mention barrier-supporting ingredients like ceramides, squalane, or fatty acids; these are commonly used to help replace the lipids dry skin runs low on.

That two-step layer — water in, then sealed — is what makes dry skin feel genuinely comfortable instead of briefly relieved.

## Occlusives at night, if you need them

If your skin is very dry, or winter air is doing its worst, add an occlusive as a final nighttime step. Occlusives — think petrolatum-based balms or rich overnight creams — form a light seal over everything underneath, slowing water loss while you sleep.

You don't need to coat your whole face. Many people use a thin layer just on the driest zones: cheeks, around the nose, lips. This step is optional and seasonal — use it when your skin asks for it, skip it when it doesn't.

## The everyday habits that dry skin out

Sometimes the biggest upgrade isn't a new product — it's removing a drying habit. The usual suspects:

- **Hot water.** A hot shower feels wonderful and strips lipids efficiently. Wash your face with lukewarm water, and try to keep your face out of the hottest spray.
- **Harsh foaming cleansers.** If it lathers like dish soap and leaves you squeaky, it's working against you.
- **Over-exfoliating.** Dry, flaky skin makes scrubs and acids tempting, but frequent exfoliation chips away at the very barrier you're rebuilding. Once a week with something gentle is plenty for most dry skin — and patch test any exfoliating acid on a small area for a few days before using it widely.
- **Over-washing.** Twice a day is a maximum, not a minimum.
- **Bone-dry indoor air.** Heated and air-conditioned rooms pull moisture from skin. A humidifier where you sleep can make a noticeable difference in comfort.

Cutting even one or two of these often does more than adding another product.

## Don't skip SPF in the morning

Dry skin still needs daily sun protection — a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every morning, all year. The good news is that sunscreen is an easy win for dry skin: cream and lotion formulas double as an extra layer of moisture, and many people find hydrating sunscreens genuinely comfortable rather than a chore. Apply it as your last morning step, over your moisturizer.

## The short version

- Dry skin care is barrier care: take less out, put more in, and seal it.
- Cleanse with a cream or milk cleanser and lukewarm water — never to the point of squeaky tightness.
- Layer hydration: humectant serum pressed into damp skin, then a cream moisturizer to lock it in.
- On very dry nights, finish with a thin occlusive layer on the driest zones.
- Cut the drying habits: hot water, harsh foaming cleansers, over-exfoliating, over-washing.
- Broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every morning — and for persistent dryness, itching, or cracking, see a board-certified dermatologist.

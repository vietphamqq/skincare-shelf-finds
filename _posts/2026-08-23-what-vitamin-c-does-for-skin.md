Vitamin C is one of the few skincare ingredients with a genuinely useful reputation and a lot of confused practice around it. People buy it, use it at night, keep it on a sunny windowsill, and then wonder why nothing much happened.

Most of that comes down to one fact about what it is: vitamin C is an antioxidant. Understanding that sorts out nearly every practical question about how to use it.

## Why it goes in the morning

Antioxidants work by neutralising free radicals — unstable molecules produced by, among other things, UV exposure and pollution. That's a defensive job, and the day is when the defending is needed.

Which is why the standard advice is to use vitamin C in the morning, after cleansing and before moisturizer and sunscreen. It's not that using it at night is harmful; it's that you're putting a daytime tool on at the wrong end of the day.

The other thing that follows: **it works alongside sunscreen, not instead of it.** An antioxidant doesn't block UV. Sunscreen is still the thing doing the heavy lifting for sun protection, and vitamin C is a supporting layer under it.

## What to expect, and what not to

Vitamin C is generally used to support a brighter, more even-looking complexion over time, and it's one of the ingredients most often paired with sunscreen in daytime routines.

What it isn't is fast, and it isn't dramatic. Weeks rather than days, and the change is the sort you notice in photographs rather than in the mirror on any given morning. Anything promising a visible transformation in a fortnight is selling rather than informing.

## How to start without irritating your skin

Some formulas — particularly higher-strength L-ascorbic acid — sting or tingle on first use, and can be too much for sensitive skin at full frequency straight away.

A reasonable approach: start with two or three mornings a week, on dry skin, and build up if it stays comfortable. Give it a few weeks before judging. If it stings persistently rather than briefly, or leaves skin red, that's a signal to drop the frequency or try a gentler derivative.

Patch test first, as with anything new — a small area, twice a day, for about a week — and introduce it on its own rather than alongside another new product.

## The storage problem

This is the part people skip. Vitamin C oxidises when exposed to light, heat and air. Oxidised vitamin C isn't dangerous, but it's much less useful — you're essentially applying an expensive nothing.

Practically: keep it in a cool, dark place rather than on a sunlit bathroom shelf, close the lid properly, prefer opaque or dark packaging, and pay attention to colour. A serum that started pale and has gone deep orange or brown has oxidised and is past its useful life, whatever the date on the box says.

Buying a smaller bottle you'll finish in a few months beats a large one that spends half its life degrading.

## Layering it with other things

Keep the routine around it simple while you're establishing whether it works for you. Sunscreen over the top, every morning, without exception — that pairing is the entire point.

If you also use strong actives at night (retinoids, acids), there's no need to double up in the morning: vitamin C in the AM and your active in the PM is the low-drama arrangement, and it makes it much easier to tell which product is responsible when something changes.

## When to check in with a professional

If vitamin C leaves persistent redness, stinging or a rash that doesn't settle after you stop, that's worth a board-certified dermatologist's opinion rather than trying another brand. The same goes for dark patches or uneven pigmentation that concern you — several causes look similar, some respond to prescription treatment, and a professional can tell them apart far faster than a shelf of serums will. And any new, changing or non-healing spot should be looked at rather than treated cosmetically.

## The short version

- Vitamin C is an antioxidant, so it belongs in the morning routine, after cleansing and before moisturizer and sunscreen.
- It works alongside sunscreen; it does not replace it and does not block UV.
- Expect gradual change over weeks, not a visible transformation in days.
- Start two or three mornings a week and build up; patch test first and introduce it on its own.
- Store it cool, dark and closed — it oxidises with light, heat and air.
- A serum that has gone deep orange or brown has oxidised; buy a size you'll actually finish.
- Vitamin C in the morning, stronger actives at night, keeps the routine simple and diagnosable.
- Persistent irritation, or pigmentation you're unsure about, is worth a board-certified dermatologist's input.

If you have spent any time in beauty corners of the internet, you have probably run into the famous "10-step Korean skincare routine." It sounds intense, a little intimidating, and honestly kind of expensive. The good news is that it is far more flexible than the name suggests. Think of the 10 steps as a menu, not a checklist you have to complete every single night. Once you understand what each step is actually for, you can build a version that fits your skin, your schedule, and your budget.

Here is the whole routine, in order, in normal language.

## Where the "10 steps" idea came from

Korean beauty culture tends to treat skincare as a slow, layered ritual rather than a quick wash-and-go. The "10 steps" became a popular shorthand for that layered approach, especially the habit of applying thin, lightweight products one after another so the skin can take in a little at a time. It is a philosophy of gentle consistency more than a rigid rulebook. Plenty of people who love this style use four or five steps on a normal day and save the full lineup for when they have time.

## The 10 steps, in order

**1. Oil cleanser.** This is the first half of "double cleansing." An oil-based cleanser or cleansing balm dissolves the oily stuff that water struggles with: sunscreen, makeup, and the natural oils your skin produces during the day. You massage it onto dry skin, then rinse or wipe away.

**2. Water-based cleanser.** The second cleanse uses a gentle foaming or gel cleanser to clear away sweat, dirt, and anything the oil cleanser left behind. Together, these two steps are meant to get skin genuinely clean without scrubbing. If you do not wear makeup or sunscreen, a single gentle cleanse is often plenty.

**3. Exfoliant.** This step helps sweep away dead surface cells that can leave skin looking dull. It is not an everyday step for most people, and using it a couple of times a week is usually enough. Overdoing exfoliation is one of the most common ways to irritate skin, so gentle and occasional beats aggressive and daily.

**4. Toner.** In Korean routines, toner is usually a hydrating, watery step that preps the skin and adds a first layer of moisture, rather than the tight, astringent toners of the past. You pat it on with your hands or a cotton pad.

**5. Essence.** Essence is a lightweight, slightly more concentrated hydrating liquid. It is one of the signature steps of Korean skincare and sits somewhere between a toner and a serum in texture. Its main job is to add hydration and help the following steps go on smoothly.

**6. Serums and ampoules.** These are your "treatment" steps, where more targeted ingredients come in for concerns like uneven tone, visible dryness, or the look of fine lines. Ampoules are simply richer, more concentrated versions of serums. You do not need several at once; one or two that suit your goals is plenty.

**7. Sheet mask.** The occasional sheet mask is a soaked cloth that delivers a big hit of hydrating ingredients while you relax for 10 to 20 minutes. This is a "sometimes" step, not a nightly one, and it is as much about the pampering as the product.

**8. Eye area product.** The skin around the eyes is thin and delicate, so many routines include a lightweight cream or gel made specifically for that area. This is an optional step, and a gentle moisturizer patted carefully around the eyes can serve a similar purpose.

**9. Moisturizer.** This is the step that seals in the hydration from everything you layered before it and helps support your skin barrier. Textures range from light gels for oilier skin to richer creams for drier skin.

**10. Sunscreen (morning) or a night cream (evening).** In the morning, the routine ends with a broad-spectrum sunscreen, which is the single most valuable daytime step for keeping skin looking its best over time. At night, you swap in a richer night cream, sleeping mask, or facial oil to lock everything in while you sleep.

## Do you actually need all 10?

Almost certainly not, and that is the reassuring part. The non-negotiables for most people are pretty short: a gentle cleanser, a moisturizer, and daily sunscreen. Everything else is a layer you can add when your skin wants more support or when you simply enjoy the ritual. Someone with oily, breakout-prone skin might skip the facial oil, while someone with very dry skin might lean into essence and a richer cream.

A realistic everyday version might look like: cleanse, a hydrating toner or essence, one serum, moisturizer, and sunscreen in the morning. That is five steps, and it captures the spirit of the routine without turning your bathroom counter into a science lab.

## A few gentle guidelines

Introduce new products one at a time rather than all at once, so that if something does not agree with your skin you can tell what caused it. Patch test any new active ingredient, such as an exfoliant or a strong serum, by applying a small amount to a discreet spot for a few days before using it on your whole face. And give any routine time; skin changes slowly, so consistency over weeks matters more than any single product. For persistent skin concerns, or if something is painful, spreading, or not settling down, it is worth seeing a board-certified dermatologist.

## The short version

- The "10-step Korean routine" is a flexible menu, not a mandatory checklist.
- The order runs from thinnest to richest: cleanse, treat and hydrate, moisturize, then protect.
- Double cleansing (oil then water) is mainly useful when you wear makeup or sunscreen.
- Exfoliating and sheet masks are occasional steps, not daily ones.
- The real essentials for most people are a gentle cleanser, a moisturizer, and daily sunscreen.
- Add new products one at a time and patch test anything active.

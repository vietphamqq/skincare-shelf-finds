Here's the short answer up front: sunscreen goes on last in your morning skincare routine — after your moisturizer, and before any makeup. Everything else about layering flows from that one rule. Below, we'll walk through the full order, how much sunscreen to actually use, and the small habits that help it sit well under everything you put on top.

## The morning order, start to finish

A simple way to remember skincare layering is thinnest to thickest: watery products first, richer ones after. Sunscreen is the one exception to the texture rule — it always closes out the skincare portion of your routine, no matter what its texture is.

Here's the full morning sequence:

1. **Cleanser** — a gentle wash, or just water if that's your preference.
2. **Toner or essence** (optional) — the lightest, most watery step.
3. **Serum** (optional) — thin treatments like a hydrating or brightening serum.
4. **Eye cream** (optional) — a small amount, patted around the orbital area.
5. **Moisturizer** — seals in the layers underneath.
6. **Sunscreen** — a broad-spectrum SPF 30+, applied generously as the final skincare step.
7. **Makeup** — primer, foundation, and everything else goes on top of sunscreen.

If your routine is shorter than this, that's completely fine. Cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen is a full and legitimate morning routine. The order is what matters, not the number of steps.

## Why sunscreen has to go last

Sunscreen works by forming a thin, even film across the surface of your skin. That film is the whole point — it's what stands between your face and the sun. Anything you rub on top of it, or mix into it, can disturb that film and leave it patchy or thinner in places than you intended.

That's why sunscreen sits at the end of the skincare sequence. Your serums and moisturizer get absorbed and settle first, giving the sunscreen a smooth, stable base to form its film on. Apply it earlier in the routine and every layer that follows would drag through it, moving product around and breaking up the coverage.

This is also why the common shortcut of blending sunscreen into your moisturizer in your palm isn't a great idea — more on that below.

## How much to use: the two-finger guideline

Most of us use far less sunscreen than we think we do, and an underdosed application simply can't do its job the way it's meant to.

A commonly used rule of thumb is the two-finger guideline: squeeze a line of sunscreen along the length of your index finger and another along your middle finger, and use that amount for your face and neck together. It will probably feel like a lot the first few times. That's a sign you've been using too little, not that the guideline is excessive.

A few application habits that help:

- Apply to clean, moisturized skin, and spread it in sections — forehead, cheeks, nose, chin, neck — rather than all at once.
- Don't forget the easy-to-miss spots: ears, hairline, jawline, and the back of the neck.
- Pat the final layer in gently instead of scrubbing it around, which helps the film stay even.

## Give it a minute before makeup

Once your sunscreen is on, let it settle before reaching for primer or foundation. A few minutes is usually enough — you're waiting for the layer to set into that even film rather than sitting wet on the surface.

When you do apply makeup, use a light touch. Patting or pressing products on with a sponge or fingertips is gentler on the sunscreen layer than dragging a brush firmly across your face. The goal is to add makeup on top of the film, not plow through it.

## Chemical vs. mineral: pick what you'll actually wear

You'll see two broad categories of sunscreen on the shelf, and for layering purposes the difference mostly comes down to texture and wear:

- **Chemical sunscreens** tend to feel lighter and more fluid, often drying down clear. Many people find them easier to wear under makeup and prefer them for oily skin days.
- **Mineral sunscreens** (the ones with zinc oxide or titanium dioxide on the label) often feel a bit creamier and can leave a subtle white or gray cast, especially on deeper skin tones — though newer tinted mineral formulas are designed to counter this.

Neither category is the "right" answer for everyone. The best sunscreen is genuinely the one you'll apply generously every morning, so it's worth trying a couple of textures to find the one that disappears into your routine rather than fighting it.

## Common layering mistakes to skip

A few habits quietly undermine even a well-ordered routine:

- **Mixing sunscreen into your moisturizer.** Blending the two in your palm dilutes the sunscreen and makes it nearly impossible to know how much you actually applied. Keep them as two separate layers.
- **Using too little.** A pea-sized dab spread thin across your whole face isn't a real application. Come back to the two-finger guideline.
- **Relying on SPF in makeup alone.** Foundations and powders with SPF are a nice bonus, but you'd need far more of them than anyone realistically wears to reach the labeled protection. Treat them as a top-up, not a substitute for a dedicated sunscreen layer.
- **Skipping the neck and ears.** They get the same sun your face does.
- **Applying to a wet face.** Let your moisturizer settle first so the sunscreen can form its film on a stable surface.

## The short version

- Sunscreen is the last step of your morning skincare routine — after moisturizer, before makeup.
- Layer thinnest to thickest, then finish with a broad-spectrum SPF 30+.
- Use the two-finger guideline for your face and neck; most people apply too little.
- Wait a few minutes after sunscreen before makeup, and pat makeup on gently.
- Chemical vs. mineral is a texture and wear preference — pick the one you'll use daily.
- Don't mix SPF into moisturizer, and don't count on makeup with SPF as your only protection.

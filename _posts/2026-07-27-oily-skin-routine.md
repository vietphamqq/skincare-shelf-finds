The best routine for oily skin is a gentle one: a gel cleanser, a lightweight moisturizer, sunscreen in the morning, and an optional BHA a few nights a week. That's it. The instinct to scrub oily skin into submission is exactly what tends to backfire.

## Why over-stripping backfires

When your skin feels greasy by noon, it's tempting to reach for the harshest cleanser on the shelf and wash until your face squeaks. But that tight, squeaky feeling isn't clean — it's a sign the wash took too much.

Strip away too much oil and skin often feels tight, uncomfortable, and irritated, and many people notice their skin looking even shinier afterward as it compensates. You can end up on a frustrating loop: harsh cleansing, discomfort, more shine, harsher cleansing. Meanwhile, an irritated skin barrier tends to make everything else in your routine sting more and work less comfortably.

A gentler approach breaks the cycle. The goal isn't zero oil — it's balanced, comfortable skin that doesn't feel like an oil slick or a desert.

## Your morning routine

Keep mornings short. Three steps cover it.

**1. A gentle gel cleanser.** Gel textures suit oily skin well: they lather lightly, rinse clean, and remove overnight oil without that stripped feeling. If your face feels tight after rinsing, the cleanser is probably too harsh. Lukewarm water, thirty seconds, done.

**2. A lightweight moisturizer.** Yes, even you. More on this myth below — but a gel or lotion moisturizer gives your skin the hydration it needs without heaviness. Look for words like "gel-cream," "oil-free," or "lightweight lotion" on the label.

**3. A broad-spectrum SPF 30+.** Sunscreen is the non-negotiable step for every skin type. Many modern formulas are made with oily skin in mind — fluid, gel, or matte-finish textures that don't feel like a film. If past sunscreens felt greasy, the texture was the problem, not sunscreen itself. Keep trying formats until one disappears into your skin.

That's the whole morning. No toner required, no ten steps.

## Your evening routine

Evenings are almost as simple, with one optional addition.

**1. Cleanse.** The same gentle gel cleanser works at night. If you wear sunscreen or makeup daily, you may find a first pass with a cleansing balm or oil helps everything come off, followed by your gel cleanser — but for many people, one thorough cleanse is enough.

**2. Optional: a BHA, a few nights a week.** Salicylic acid (a BHA) is commonly used in routines for oily skin because it's oil-soluble, which means it can get into pores where oil sits. Many people find regular use helps the look of clogged pores and rough texture. Start with two or three nights a week, not nightly — more is not better, and overuse is one of the fastest routes back to the stripped, irritated skin we're trying to avoid. Patch test on a small area for a few days before using it on your whole face.

**3. Moisturize.** The same lightweight moisturizer from the morning, or a slightly more nourishing lotion if your skin runs drier at night. Never skip this on BHA nights — hydrated skin handles actives more comfortably.

## The myth that won't die: "oily skin doesn't need moisturizer"

Oil and hydration are different things. Oil is the sebum your skin produces; hydration is the water content within your skin. Oily skin can absolutely be under-hydrated — and when it is, it often feels tight and looks shiny at the same time, which convinces people to skip moisturizer even harder.

Skipping moisturizer doesn't teach your skin to produce less oil. It just leaves skin dehydrated and uncomfortable, and many people find their shine looks worse for it. A lightweight moisturizer supports your skin barrier, keeps hydration in, and makes the rest of your routine — especially any BHA — far easier to tolerate.

## Choose textures, not just ingredients

For oily skin, texture is half the battle. The right ingredient in the wrong texture will sit heavily and end up abandoned in a drawer.

- **Cleanser:** gel or light foam over creamy, milky washes
- **Moisturizer:** gel or gel-cream over rich, buttery creams
- **Sunscreen:** fluid, gel, or matte-finish over thick, classic beach formulas
- **Serums (if you add any later):** watery or gel textures over facial oils

Heavy creams aren't "bad" — they're simply built for a different skin type. If a product feels like a coat you can't take off, it's not the one for you, no matter how loved it is. ## The short version

- Harsh, stripping routines backfire — many people find their skin looks shinier and feels worse after over-cleansing.
- Morning: gentle gel cleanser, lightweight gel or lotion moisturizer, broad-spectrum SPF 30+.
- Evening: cleanse, an optional BHA two to three nights a week, then moisturizer — and patch test the BHA first.
- Oily skin still needs moisturizer; oil and hydration are not the same thing.
- Pick lighter textures across the board: gels and lotions over heavy creams.
- For persistent breakouts or concerns that don't improve, see a board-certified dermatologist.

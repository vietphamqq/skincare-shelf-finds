Slugging is one of the few internet skincare trends that turns out to be an old dermatology habit with a new name. Strip away the branding and it's this: after your evening routine, you finish with a thin layer of an occlusive — usually petrolatum or a balm built around it — and go to bed.

The name comes from the finish, which is glossy in a way that photographs badly and works well.

## What it actually does

Moisturizing ingredients fall into three broad groups. Humectants draw water into the outer layer of skin. Emollients smooth the surface. Occlusives sit on top and slow how fast water evaporates away.

Occlusives are the most straightforwardly effective of the three at reducing water loss, and petrolatum is the most effective occlusive commonly available. That's the whole mechanism of slugging: less water escapes overnight, so skin is more comfortable and less tight in the morning.

Two things follow from that, and they're the two things people most often get wrong.

The first: **an occlusive seals moisture in; it doesn't add any.** Slugging over dry, bare skin traps not very much. It belongs on top of a moisturizer, not instead of one, and ideally on skin that's still slightly damp.

The second: **it seals in whatever else is on your face.** That's fine over a plain moisturizer. It's a bad idea over a retinoid or a strong acid, where pushing more of an active into skin than intended is how you get irritation you weren't expecting.

## Who tends to benefit

**Dry skin, especially in dry air.** Winter, heated rooms, air conditioning, dry climates. This is the clearest case and the one where people notice a difference on the first morning.

**Skin that's temporarily compromised.** After a stretch of over-exfoliating, wind burn, or a period where the barrier feels raw, a plain occlusive over a plain moisturizer is a comfortable, low-risk way to leave things alone while they settle.

**Specific dry patches.** Lips, the sides of the nose, around the eyes, hands, heels. Slugging one area is often more useful than slugging your whole face.

## Who should be careful

**Acne-prone skin.** Petrolatum itself is generally considered non-comedogenic, but sealing a layer over skin that's already congested, or over a heavy cream, can make breakouts worse for some people. If you want to try it, start with a small area for a week and see.

**Oily skin.** Usually there's nothing much to gain, and the finish is unpleasant when your skin is already producing plenty of its own oil.

**Anyone using strong actives at night.** Skip slugging on those evenings, or use it on the alternate nights instead.

## How to do it

Cleanse. Apply your usual serum and moisturizer while skin is still slightly damp. Wait a few minutes so nothing is pooling on the surface. Then apply a thin layer of an occlusive balm — a small amount, spread until it's barely there rather than sitting in a visible film.

Use a clean pillowcase, and expect some transfer. In the morning, wash your face as usual; a gentle cleanser removes it fine.

Two or three nights a week is a reasonable starting point. Nightly is unnecessary for most people, and doing it on the nights you use actives is the main thing to avoid.

## When to check in with a professional

If you're slugging because your skin is persistently dry, tight or flaky no matter what you use, that's worth investigating rather than sealing over — eczema, an underactive thyroid and a number of medications all cause dryness that doesn't respond to a better bedtime routine. A board-certified dermatologist can tell the difference. The same applies if slugging brings on breakouts that don't settle after you stop, or if you develop redness, itching or a rash in the area.

## The short version

- Slugging means finishing an evening routine with a thin layer of an occlusive balm, usually petrolatum-based.
- It slows water loss overnight; it seals moisture in rather than adding any, so it goes over a moisturizer, not instead of one.
- Apply to slightly damp skin, wait a few minutes, then use a thin layer — barely visible, not a thick film.
- Best for dry skin, dry air, temporarily compromised skin, and specific dry patches like lips and around the nose.
- Skip it over retinoids and strong acids; sealing those in can cause irritation.
- Acne-prone and oily skin should test a small area first, or skip it entirely.
- Persistent dryness, or breakouts that don't settle after stopping, are worth a board-certified dermatologist's opinion.
